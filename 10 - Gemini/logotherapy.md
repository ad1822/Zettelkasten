### AI Summary

Logotherapy is a school of psychotherapy developed by Austrian neurologist and psychiatrist Viktor Frankl, often referred to as the "Third Viennese School of Psychotherapy" after Freud's psychoanalysis and Adler's individual psychology.[1][2] The term "logotherapy" comes from the Greek word "logos," meaning "meaning," and its core premise is that the primary motivational force in humans is the search for meaning in life.[3][4][1] Frankl developed this theory prior to his experiences in Nazi concentration camps, which later served as a profound confirmation of his ideas.[3][5][6]

**Core Principles:**
Logotherapy is founded on several key principles:
*   **Freedom of Will:** Individuals have the freedom to choose their response to life's circumstances and are personally responsible for their choices.[3][7]
*   **Will to Meaning:** Humans are driven by an inherent desire to find purpose and meaning in life. When this search is thwarted, it can lead to existential frustration and feelings of meaninglessness, also known as the "existential vacuum."[3][5][7]
*   **Meaning of Life:** Life has meaning under all circumstances, even the most undesirable or suffering ones.[3][1] Meaning can be discovered through creating a work or doing a deed, experiencing something or encountering someone (e.g., love), or by the attitude one takes toward unavoidable suffering.[1][8]

**Techniques:**
Logotherapy utilizes specific techniques to help individuals find meaning and cope with struggles:
*   **Dereflection:** This technique helps individuals shift their focus away from their problems or symptoms by redirecting their attention towards meaningful goals or activities, reducing self-preoccupation.[8][9]
*   **Paradoxical Intention:** Clients are encouraged to intend or wish for precisely what they fear, which can be particularly useful for obsessive, compulsive, and phobic conditions, as well as anticipatory anxiety.[5][6][9]
*   **Socratic Dialogue:** Through exploratory conversations and open-ended questions, the therapist guides the client to uncover their underlying values, beliefs, and sources of meaning, facilitating self-discovery.[3][9]

**Effectiveness and Applications:**
Logotherapy is a future-oriented approach that helps people endure hardship and suffering by finding purpose.[5] Research suggests that having a meaning or purpose in life is connected to overall health, happiness, and life satisfaction, and can enhance resilience.[5][7] Logotherapy has been shown to improve people's sense of meaning and can be effective in reducing depression in children, improving the quality of life for adolescents with cancer, and reducing job burnout.[5] It emphasizes the importance of self-transcendence, connecting with something greater than oneself, and finding significance in relationships and contributions to society.[8][10]
