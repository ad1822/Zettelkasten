# Grammar rules

### I have vs I do have

- First, Both sentences are right

- **Neutral statement**: "I have" is the simple, unmarked form used for straightforward facts.
- Example: I have a car. I have three sisters.

- **Emphatic/contrastive**: "I do have" adds emphasis or correction, often to contradict, reassure, insist, or respond to doubt.
- Example: I do have a car (contradicting "You don't have a car"). I do have time to help you.

### Might be vs Maybe

"May be" and "might be" both express possibility, but "might be" suggests a lower or less certain probability than "may be". In many cases, they are used interchangeably, especially in American English, but "may be" can also be used to ask for or give permission, a function that "might be" does not have. 

#### Key differences

| Feature            | May be                                                                                       | Might be                                                                              |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Probability        | Suggests a more likely possibility (e.g., "She may be at home").                             | Suggests a less likely or more hypothetical possibility (e.g., "You might be right"). |
| Permission         | Can be used to ask for or give permission.                                                   | Is not used for permission.                                                           |
| Formal vs.         | Can sound more formal.                                                                       | Can be more informal.                                                                 |
| Interchangeability | Often interchangeable in casual conversation, but the difference in certainty can be subtle. | Often interchangeable with "may be," but often suggests a lower probability.          |
#### Examples

- Possibility:
    - May be: "It may rain later" (a higher probability of rain). 
    - Might be: "If I buy a lottery ticket, I might win" (a lower, hypothetical probability). 
- Permission:
    - May be: "May I be excused from class?". 
    - Might be: (Not used for permission). 
- Past tense:
    - May be: "He may have forgotten his wallet". 
    - Might be: "He might have forgotten his wallet".
