# Hope is fucked

### Talking too much about yourself can also be a means to conceal yourself

---
### Instead of hope

Nietzsche instead believed that **we must look beyond hope**.  For him, this morality of the future had to begin must look beyond values. We must evolve into something "beyond with something he called Amor fate, or “love of one's fate”: “My formula for greatness in a human being,” he wrote, “is Amor fate they in all eternity. Not merely bear what is necessary, still less conceal, one wants nothing to be different, not forward. not backward, it-all idealism is mendacity in the face of what is necessary-but love it.”

Amor fate, for Nietzsche, meant the **unconditional acceptance of all life and experience**: the highs and the lows, the meaning and the meaninglessness. It meant loving one's pain, embracing one's suffering. It meant closing the separation between one's desires and reality not by striving for more desires, but by simply desiring reality.

**hope for nothing**. **Hope for what already is-because hope is ultimately empty**. Anything your mind can conceptualize is fundamentally flawed and limited and therefore damaging if worshiped unconditionally. Don't hope for more happiness. Don't hope for less suffering. Don't hope to improve your character. Don't hope to eliminate your flaws.

Hope for this. Hope for the infinite opportunity and oppression present in every single moment. Hope for the suffering that comes with freedom. For the pain that comes from happiness. For the wisdom that comes from ignorance. For the power that comes from surrender.

### Summary

Friedrich Nietzsche's philosophy offers a radical perspective on hope, intertwined with his concept of *amor fati* (love of fate). He famously stated, "Hope in reality is the worst of all evils because it prolongs the torments of man." For Nietzsche, hope can be a trap that prevents us from fully embracing the present moment, with all its suffering and joy. It can become a reason to avoid taking action and to passively wait for a better future that may never come.

This is where *amor fati* comes in. It is the practice of not just accepting, but loving everything that happens in one's life. It's about saying "yes" to life, unconditionally. This includes embracing pain and suffering as essential components of a meaningful existence. By loving our fate, we are no longer dependent on hope for a better future. We find meaning and fulfillment in the here and now, regardless of the circumstances.

For Nietzsche, the ultimate goal is to become an *Übermensch* (Overman), a self-mastered individual who creates their own values and lives life to the fullest. The Übermensch does not need hope because they have the strength to affirm life in its entirety. They have embraced *amor fati*.
