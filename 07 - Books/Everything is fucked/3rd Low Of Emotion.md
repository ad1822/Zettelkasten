# 3rd Law Of Emotion

 **“Your Identity Will Stay Your Identity Until a New Experience Acts Against It.”** This law posits that our values are not merely a collection of feelings, but rather deeply ingrained stories or narratives that we construct about ourselves. Once these narratives are formed, we tend to hold onto them tightly, and they become fundamental to our identity.

The core idea is that significant personal change, particularly in our values and identity, can only occur when we encounter new experiences that directly challenge or contradict these established narratives. This process is often uncomfortable and painful, as Manson emphasizes that “there's no such thing as change without pain” and “no growth without discomfort.” To truly become a new version of oneself, one must first grieve the loss of the person they used to be.

For instance, if someone develops a belief that “all boys are shit” due to past negative experiences, this belief becomes part of their identity. This narrative will persist until a new, contradictory experience—such as meeting a genuinely kind and respectful man—forces them to re-evaluate and potentially change their deeply held value. To heal and replace unhelpful values, Manson suggests revisiting experiences and actively re-writing the narratives associated with them.
