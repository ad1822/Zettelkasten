# 2nd Law Of Emotion

 **“Our self-worth equals the sum of our emotions over time.”** This law suggests that our sense of self-worth is not a fixed trait but rather an accumulation of our emotional experiences and how we interpret them throughout our lives. As individuals encounter various situations, they form judgments about themselves based on these occurrences.

Key aspects of the 2nd Law of Emotion include:

*   **Impact of Experiences:** Repeated negative experiences or perceived failures can lead to an internalization of these events, causing individuals to believe they deserve such outcomes, which in turn diminishes their self-worth.
*   **Inflated Self-Worth:** Conversely, consistent positive reinforcement or praise, even if unearned, can lead to an inflated sense of self-worth and narcissism.
*   **Narcissism and Hope:** Manson posits that a degree of inherent narcissism and self-delusion about one's specialness is common, serving as a source of hope. However, this can also be rooted in insecurity.
*   **Internalization of Moral Gaps:** The 2nd Law builds upon the concept of “moral gaps” from the 1st Law of Emotion. When a perceived injustice or imbalance (a moral gap) remains unresolved, individuals tend to internalize it, which directly impacts their self-worth.
*   **Self-Perception:** Ultimately, this law underscores the critical role of our internal narrative and how we perceive ourselves in shaping our overall self-worth.
